# Dakshinamoorthy and Navgraha EME Temple

**ID:** `dakshinamurti_eme_temple`

**Aliases:** EME Temple, Dakshinamurti Temple

## Overview
- **Category:** religious heritage
- **Subcategory:** Hindu temple; military-era modern temple
- **Location:** Inside EME School campus, EME Road, Fatehgunj, Vadodara, Gujarat 390002
- **Coordinates:** 22.3314, 73.192
- **Historical period:** Mid-20th century, post-Independence India
- **Built / construction date:** 1965–1966; completed and consecrated in February 1966

## Historical Context
Constructed by the Electronics & Mechanical Engineers (EME) School of the Indian Army and maintained and run by the Army since inception. It was designed and built under the leadership of Brigadier A.F. Eugene, who was a Christian. The source describes this background as central to the temple's narrative as a symbol of secularism and inter-faith harmony within the armed forces. Tourism and heritage write-ups often describe it as one of the first or the first army-built temples in the world, but the source material presents this as a commonly used description rather than a definitively established fact.

## Associated People
- Brigadier A.F. Eugene, first Commandant of EME School

## Architecture
**Style:** Modern dome-shaped design inspired by Buckminster Fuller

**Materials**
- Aluminium framework
- Aluminium sheets

**Notable Features**
- Dome-shaped structure
- Kalasha representing Hinduism
- Dome representing Islamic mosque style
- Tower/spire associated in the design with Christianity
- Golden structure above the tower associated with Buddhism
- Entrance design associated with Jainism
- Havan kund associated with Zoroastrianism
- Main Dakshinamurti idol faces south
- Ganesha and Kartikeya idols, said to have been brought from Mahabalipuram
- Silver arch behind the Shiva idol engraved with 'Om Namah Shivaya'
- Replica of the Amarnath Shivalinga
- Over 100 ancient stone statues from approximately the 6th–16th centuries CE in the gardens
- Navgraha temple added in 2023 as a scale replica of the main structure, with nine planetary idols and a Shani Shila

## Cultural Significance
Functions as a living place of worship for soldiers, staff and local devotees, while also serving as a tourist and educational site for architecture and heritage enthusiasts. It is locally celebrated as a symbol of unity in diversity and religious harmony. Maha Shivratri and Janmashtami attract large crowds. The ancient sculptures connect the modern military institution with broader Indian art-historical traditions.

## Religious Significance
A Hindu temple dedicated to Lord Shiva in the Dakshinamurti form, understood as Shiva as supreme teacher. The idol represents Shiva as a teacher who gives knowledge and wisdom. The complex also contains a Navgraha shrine added in 2023.

## Historical Events
- Construction during 1965–1966
- Consecration in February 1966
- Navgraha temple added in 2023
- Maha Shivratri and Janmashtami celebrations draw large crowds

## Interesting Facts
- It is a Hindu temple built and managed by the Indian Army inside a military school campus.
- Its design incorporates symbols associated with multiple religions.
- It combines a modern 1960s structure with ancient sculptures in its gardens.
- Its aluminium dome is unusual compared with traditional Hindu temple designs in Gujarat.

## Lesser-Known Facts
- The temple is less famous than Laxmi Vilas Palace and is therefore overlooked by many tourists.
- Its location inside an army cantonment can lead visitors to assume that entry is difficult or prohibited.
- The gardens contain ancient stone statues dating approximately from the 6th to 16th centuries CE.

## Stories and Legends
Not specified in the source material.

## Current Condition
The temple is described as well-maintained and clean, with green gardens. Daily prayers and worship continue. The old statues are kept in open garden areas. The 2023 Navgraha addition indicates continued use, care and development by the Army and EME School.

## Visitor Information
- **Timings:** Commonly reported as 6:00/6:30 AM to 8:30/9:00 PM daily; special Tuesday aarti around 6:45 PM.
- **Entry fee:** Free; no ticket required.
- **Accessibility / how to reach:** Inside EME School campus. About 5 km from Vadodara Junction railway station; reachable by auto, taxi or city bus to Fatehgunj/EME Road. Nearest airport is Vadodara International Airport.
- **Restrictions / etiquette:** Visitors may need to show valid photo ID at the Army-campus gate. Foreign nationals may need special permission or may not be allowed. Modest clothing is recommended and footwear should be removed before entering sanctum areas.

## Nearby Places
Not specified in the source material.

## Tourism Value
Useful for visitors interested in modern heritage, military history, architecture, religious symbolism, inter-faith harmony and ancient sculpture collections.

## Hidden Gem

# Sevasi Vav

**ID:** `sevasi_vav`

**Aliases:** Sevasi Vaav, Vidhyadhar Vav, Vidhyadhar ni Vav, Aakota Stepwell

## Overview
- **Category:** water heritage
- **Subcategory:** historic stepwell (vav)
- **Location:** Sevasi Vav (Vidhyadhar Vav), near main Sevasi bus stop, Sevasi–Sindhrot road, Vadodara, Gujarat 391101
- **Coordinates:** 22.3182, 73.1188
- **Historical period:** Late 15th–mid-16th century; Sultanate-period Vadodara region
- **Built / construction date:** Generally dated to the late 15th–mid-16th century; many sources place it around c. 1496–1543 CE

## Historical Context
The stepwell is described as having been built in memory of Vidyadhar, who died young. It is believed that the state treasury provided the money for its construction. It formed part of a network of more than 100 stepwells around Vadodara that stored rainwater and provided drinking water. The source places its construction during the time of Sultan Mahmud Begada / Muzaffarid Gujarat Sultanate.

## Associated People
- Vidyadhar, a respected local spiritual leader

## Architecture
**Style:** Historic Gujarat Sultanate-period stepwell architecture

**Materials**
- Brick
- Stone

**Notable Features**
- East-to-west orientation with entrance on the west
- Seven underground levels
- Long stepped stairway descending toward the well
- Large arches along the stairway
- Five pavilion towers
- Five supporting structures
- Entrance carvings of two tigers on the left and two elephants on the right
- Carvings of flowers, bells, animals and toran patterns
- Figures including horses with riders and geese
- Old writings and inscriptions relating to festivals and community life
- Domed structure at one end providing a balcony/viewing platform
- Well shaft extending to the water table

## Cultural Significance
The stepwell was historically an important water source and community meeting place, especially for women. It remains associated with small religious activities, with prayer flags, coconuts, diyas and small shrines visible inside.

## Religious Significance
Small religious activities continue at the site. Prayer flags, coconuts, diyas and small shrines are present.

## Historical Events
Not specified in the source material.

## Interesting Facts
- It is a seven-level underground stepwell.
- Its entrance has distinctive tiger and elephant carvings.
- It combines a roughly 500-year-old water structure with surviving decorative carvings and a domed viewing structure.
- It was historically part of a larger network of more than 100 stepwells around Vadodara.

## Lesser-Known Facts
- It is hidden behind shops and lacks clear signboards.
- It is less famous than Rani ki Vav, Adalaj Stepwell and Laxmi Vilas Palace.
- Artists and media have highlighted it through awareness efforts such as 'Soul of the Vav'.

## Stories and Legends
- **Legend:** A local folk story claims that young girls wearing gold were sacrificed at the stepwell on full-moon nights for prosperity. The source explicitly states that this is only a folk story and is not proven history.

## Current Condition
The stepwell is mostly still standing but is described as poorly maintained. Some bricks are exposed and plants have grown on the steps. Lower levels have stagnant, foul-smelling water. Some paintings and carvings are damaged by moisture, chemicals and vandalism. The dome and upper levels are in better condition. Littering and misuse are identified as heritage threats. As of 2024–2025, the source says it had not received major government restoration work comparable to Rani ki Vav.

## Visitor Information
- **Timings:** No official ticket counter or fixed hours; described as effectively open 24 hours, with daylight visits strongly recommended.
- **Entry fee:** Free; no ticket.
- **Accessibility / how to reach:** Near the main Sevasi bus stop on the Sevasi–Sindhrot road. Accessible by auto/taxi; city buses toward Sevasi/Sindhrot can be used, followed by a short walk.
- **Restrictions / etiquette:** Lower levels may be dark, damp and unsafe. Visitors are advised to watch their step, avoid climbing on fragile carvings, not litter or throw anything into the well, and respect local religious activity. Modest clothing is advisable.

## Nearby Places
Not specified in the source material.

## Tourism Value
Strong interest for visitors focused on historic water systems, stepwell architecture, sculpture, photography, local community history and lesser-known heritage.

## Hidden Gem
Yes

## Data Quality Notes
- The source gives an approximate construction period and a range of c. 1496–1543 CE rather than one verified construction date.
- The Aakota Stepwell label appears in the section heading, while the place is otherwise consistently described as Sevasi Vav/Vidhyadhar Vav. The source does not explicitly explain the naming relationship.
- Some visitor information is based on secondary sources and should not be treated as official site policy.

# Khanderao Market

**ID:** `khanderao_market`

**Aliases:** Khande Rao Market

## Overview
- **Category:** civic heritage
- **Subcategory:** historic market and municipal building
- **Location:** Khanderao Market, Chamaraja Road / Palace Road, near old city and Laxmi Vilas Palace, Vadodara, Gujarat 390001
- **Coordinates:** 22.2966, 73.2015
- **Historical period:** Early 20th century; late colonial / princely-state era
- **Built / construction date:** 1906–1907

## Historical Context
Commissioned by Maharaja Sayajirao Gaekwad III to mark the silver jubilee of his administration and gifted to the city municipality as a civic and commercial asset. The market was originally a major wholesale and retail market for vegetables, fruits and flowers, with 483 shops and about 300 platforms (otlas). It was one of the last major buildings designed by British architect Robert Chisholm in India.

## Associated People
- Maharaja Sayajirao Gaekwad III
- Maharaja Khanderao Gaekwad II
- Robert Chisholm
- Vinayak Pandurang Karmarkar (V.R. Karmarkar)

## Architecture
**Style:** Indo-Saracenic

**Materials**
- Bricks
- Mortar
- Dhrangadhra stone
- Indian patent stone

**Notable Features**
- Two-storey U-shaped plan
- Large open courtyard with a central fountain
- Two long bays, each about 70 ft × 34 ft
- Overhead passage connecting the bays
- Central dome approximately 86 ft high
- Entrance inspired by Dabhoi Fort gate and Lehripura Gate
- Hindu-style spires (shikharas)
- Decorative brackets
- Projecting windows (jharokhas)
- Domes influenced by Hindu temple designs
- Arches showing Islamic architectural influence
- Stone jali screens similar to Mughal examples
- Clock tower
- Marble statue of Maharaja Khanderao Gaekwad II in the courtyard

## Cultural Significance
The building remains a working market and houses Vadodara Municipal Corporation offices, combining historical architecture with everyday civic and commercial life. Its courtyard and shops reflect traditional bazaar culture, including shopping, bargaining, festivals and social interaction. It forms part of Vadodara's Indo-Saracenic architectural identity.

## Religious Significance
Not specified in the source material.

## Historical Events
- Construction in 1906–1907
- Built to mark Sayajirao Gaekwad III's 25-year silver jubilee
- Marble statue of Maharaja Khanderao Gaekwad II inaugurated by Sayajirao on 23 December 1935

## Interesting Facts
- It looks like a grand palace but functions as a market and government building.
- The building was designed by Robert Chisholm.
- It originally had 483 shops and about 300 platforms (otlas).
- The main entrance draws inspiration from historic Vadodara and Dabhoi gates.

## Lesser-Known Facts
- It is often overshadowed by Laxmi Vilas Palace and other major Vadodara attractions.
- Visitors often encounter it primarily as a busy market rather than as a heritage building.
- The building was gifted to the municipality as a civic and commercial asset.

## Stories and Legends
Not specified in the source material.

## Current Condition
The building is mostly intact and in fairly good condition and remains in active use as a market and VMC office. The stone has darkened over time, some areas have plant growth, and normal wear is visible from heavy daily use. The surrounding area can be busy, noisy and crowded. Some surrounding parts have been modified or occupied, while the main historic structure remains clearly visible and in use.

## Visitor Information
- **Timings:** Market area generally operates around 7:00 AM–8:30 PM, with individual stalls varying. VMC offices typically operate 10:00 AM–5:00 PM, Monday–Saturday, closed Sundays and public holidays.
- **Entry fee:** Free to enter the market area and courtyard; no ticket.
- **Accessibility / how to reach:** Well-known landmark on Palace Road. Reachable by auto/taxi; nearby city buses serve Laxmi Vilas Palace, Mandvi and old-city stops.
- **Restrictions / etiquette:** Parking is limited and often paid. Visitors should be mindful of crowds, vendors and moving carts and keep valuables secure.

## Nearby Places
- Nyaya Mandir (nearby)
- Raopura Clock Tower (nearby)
- Laxmi Vilas Palace (nearby)

## Tourism Value
Useful for visitors interested in Indo-Saracenic architecture, princely-state urban planning, civic history and living bazaar culture.

## Hidden Gem
Yes

## Data Quality Notes
- The source describes the market as one of the last major buildings designed by Robert Chisholm in India and also states that the design was prepared before he left India; this is preserved without adding further interpretation.
- Some visitor information is attributed in the source to travel-guide material rather than an official heritage-site schedule.

# Makarpura Palace

**ID:** `makarpura_palace`

**Aliases:** None specified

## Overview
- **Category:** royal heritage
- **Subcategory:** palace; former summer residence and hunting lodge
- **Location:** Himmat Nagar, Makarpura Road, Vadodara, Gujarat 390010
- **Coordinates:** 22.2412, 73.1949
- **Historical period:** 19th-century British Colonial / Princely State era
- **Built / construction date:** Originally built in 1870; extended and renovated between 1883 and 1890

## Historical Context
Built as a royal summer residence and hunting lodge for the Gaekwad dynasty. Maharaja Khanderao Gaekwad commissioned the original structure in 1870 because of its proximity to deer-rich hunting grounds (Shikarkhana) in Dhaniavi. Maharaja Sayajirao Gaekwad III later extended and renovated it under the guidance of British architect Robert Fellowes Chisholm. It later served as a filming location for early Indian silent cinema, including Prithvi Vallabh (1924). After Independence it was repurposed and is currently described as an active military defense facility, No. 17 Tetra School training facility for the Indian Air Force.

## Associated People
- Maharaja Khanderao Gaekwad
- Maharaja Sayajirao Gaekwad III
- Robert Fellowes Chisholm
- William Goldring

## Architecture
**Style:** Classical Italian Renaissance architectural style

**Materials**
Not specified in the source material.

**Notable Features**
- Three-storey double-wing layout
- Original 1870 structure on the right
- Matching 1880s extension on the left
- Ground and upper-floor corridors connecting the wings
- Rows of classical arches decreasing in size on higher floors
- Single-storey porte-cochère
- Multifoil Italian fountains at the entrance
- Over 100 rooms
- Frame-arch balconies
- Wooden staircases
- Painted ceilings
- Stucco panels
- Originally a 130-acre Japanese-style garden designed by William Goldring
- Pavilions, fountains and an artificial lake in the original garden

## Cultural Significance
Represents a major example of Italian Renaissance revivalist design commissioned by a 19th-century Indian princely state. It highlights the architectural contribution of Robert Fellowes Chisholm to Baroda's royal landscape.

## Religious Significance
Not specified in the source material.

## Historical Events
- Original palace commissioned and built in 1870
- Extension and renovation between 1883 and 1890
- Used as a filming location for Prithvi Vallabh (1924)
- Repurposed after Independence as a military facility

## Interesting Facts
- Its design is described as almost purely European Italian Renaissance style, unlike many Gaekwad structures associated with Indo-Saracenic design.
- The two grand structural wings were built decades apart under two different Maharajas.
- The original grounds included a 130-acre Japanese-style garden.

## Lesser-Known Facts
- The palace is largely inaccessible to the general public because it functions as an operational defense campus.
- The original garden was designed by William Goldring, associated in the source with the Royal Botanic Gardens.

## Stories and Legends
Not specified in the source material.

## Current Condition
Maintained under Indian Air Force custodianship. The outer structure, main facades and surrounding grounds remain intact, while internal spaces reflect modifications for administrative use.

## Visitor Information
- **Timings:** Restricted military area; viewable only from outside the main perimeter gates.
- **Entry fee:** N/A; closed to the general public.
- **Accessibility / how to reach:** Exterior roadside viewing from Makarpura Road. Approximately 9 km south of Vadodara Junction and approximately 13 km from Vadodara International Airport. Accessible by local buses, auto-rickshaws or taxis.
- **Restrictions / etiquette:** Strict military security rules apply. Unauthorized entry, drone flying and photography of security gates are prohibited. Foreign visitors are subject to military security access restrictions.

## Nearby Places
Not specified in the source material.

## Tourism Value
Primarily valuable as an architectural and historical exterior-viewing landmark for visitors interested in Gaekwad-era royal architecture, European revival styles and the later military reuse of heritage buildings.

## Hidden Gem

## Data Quality Notes
- The source does not provide a current public-access route beyond exterior viewing and explicitly describes the site as a restricted military area.
- The palace's current use is described as an Indian Air Force training facility in the source material; no further current operational details are added.
